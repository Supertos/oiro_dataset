Evening. Harold sits on his chair at the top of the tower observing magnificent sunset. Sun settles down, its orange aura tints blue sky. The top of this firmament is hinted dark blue - almost black, with the first stars beginning to twinkle. Harold inhales fresh forest air, cicadas buzzing in tall grass of fort's exterior.

Clip-clop. Clip-clop.
Harold hears as carriage with Princess arrives. Trumpets play and gate opens for her majesty's transport. Harold doesn't get up - it's none of his business, after all. Trumpets keep playing, confetti keeps falling, and only Harold's oblivious to all of this Parade.
He's not the knight of the princess;
He's not the nobleman;
He's not even a peasant child, to whom the Princess would smile beautifully.

Harold closes his eyes. Soon, trumpets fall silent, and soothing song of cicadas return to caress Harold's tired ears. After all, he's turning 45 this month. A quiet life will meet a quiet end, and the new Harold will sit on the same chair, watching new Princess, new Carriage, new Parade unravel.
